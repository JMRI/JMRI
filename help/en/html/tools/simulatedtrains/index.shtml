JMRI: Simulated Trains

Simulated trains are virtual trains that run on your layout's track as JMRI knows it, with no layout connected. They move when JMRI sends them speed commands, as real trains would, and set occupancy sensors as they pass, so Dispatcher, Warrants, Logix, LogixNG, scripts and panels see them as they would see real trains. The simulator also keeps the truth of where each train really is, which can be compared with what JMRI concluded.

Only in a simulated profile

A train can only be placed in a profile whose connections are all simulators: the Internal connection, and the simulator connections of the hardware systems, such as CAN Simulation. Simulated trains can therefore never show occupancy on a real layout. If a real connection is added while trains are placed, they are all removed, with a warning.

How they are driven and seen

  • Simulated trains obey the Internal connection's throttles, by DCC address. A throttle window, Dispatcher, a Warrant or a script using that connection drives them with no further set-up.
  • They set internal sensors. Give each Block an internal sensor as its occupancy sensor; a Block's sensor goes active when the first part of a train that detectors see enters it, and inactive when the last of every train has left it. A sensor of another connection is left alone.
  • Two trains that touch both stop. A train whose turnout is moved under it derails and stays where it stopped.

The track they run on

The simulator works out its own routes from the layout's configuration:

  • a Block drawn on a Layout Editor panel is followed on the drawing, with its turnouts as they are set;
  • an OBlock is followed through its Portals and paths;
  • any other Block is followed by its own paths, each of which names the Block it leads to.

Block lengths matter: a train takes as long to cross a Block as its length and speed say.

The trains

A simulated train has a name, its locos (the first is at the front), its length, and how detectors see it: the whole train, only the loco, or the loco and a marker at the rear, such as a resistor wheelset.

A loco can be described from its roster entry. Its speed then comes from the measured steps of its speed profile, and its momentum from CV3 and CV4 in its roster file (with CV23 and CV24 in a consist). Without a profile, its speed is in proportion to the throttle setting, up to a scale 100 mph at the layout scale set in the Warrant preferences.

Placing a train

There is no window for simulated trains yet: they are placed by scripts. The sample script SimulatedTrains.py (Scripting ⇒ Run Script..., in a profile whose connections are all simulators, such as one with only the Internal connection) builds a short line of five Blocks, places two trains on it, drives them with Internal throttles, and prints after each move what each sensor reports beside which trains are really in each Block. It removes its trains at the end.

In a script of your own, a train is described and placed like this:

import jmri
from jmri.jmrit.simulation import Detection, SimulatedLayout, SimulatedLoco, VirtualTrain

loco = SimulatedLoco.builder(jmri.DccLocoAddress(3, False)).length(150).build()
train = VirtualTrain.builder("Goods").loco(loco).length(400).detection(Detection.entireTrain()).build()
layout = SimulatedLayout.getDefault()
# front 200 mm into Block B2, having come from Block B1
layout.place(train, blocks.getBlock("B2"), blocks.getBlock("B1"), 200)
...
layout.remove(train)

For a loco in the roster, use SimulatedLoco.builder(entry) with its roster entry instead of its address.

What they are for

  • Trying out Dispatcher, Warrants, Logix, LogixNG, scripts and panels before running them on the layout, or when the layout isn't to hand.
  • Showing how a layout's automation works, with trains moving on its panels.
  • Testing JMRI itself. Tests and scripts can also set faults, such as a detector that misses a train for a moment, a sensor showing a train that isn't there, two sensors wired to each other's Blocks, a Block longer or shorter than JMRI has it, a train that stalls, or a train that parts.
