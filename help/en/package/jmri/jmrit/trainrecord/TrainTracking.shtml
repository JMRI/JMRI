JMRI: Train Tracking

Train tracking follows each train from Block to Block, works out where it is between detectors, and explains what it has found. It only watches: it doesn't set turnouts or signals, hold Blocks, or stop trains, and Dispatcher, Warrants and throttles carry on as they are.

Which trains are followed

A train is followed once it has been placed: when Dispatcher starts an Active Train in an occupied Block, or when a Block's value is set to the train (its roster entry, ID tag or name). From then on, each time a Block next to one of the train's ends becomes occupied along a path that is set, the train has entered it; when a Block at its rear becomes empty, it has left it. A Block with no occupancy sensor has no detector: a train is followed through it when it is seen in the next detected Block.

For each train, train tracking records how sure it is of where the train is:

  • Confirmed: just seen by a detector, or placed.
  • Position estimated: between detectors, or not seen since a detector dropped out.
  • Ambiguous: one of several trains that could have entered a Block.
  • Stalled: told to move, but not seen to reach the next Block in time.
  • Lost: expected to have reached the next Block, with nothing telling it to stop.

Warnings

While trains are followed, a window opens when a train stalls or is lost. Show Me opens the Block dialog; Later closes the window, and the warning stays in Tools > Train Tracking > Warnings until it is over. To stop these windows opening, use Preferences > Messages > Train Tracking Warnings. Train tracking only warns: no train is stopped.

A Block that becomes occupied when no train could have entered it opens no window: it is listed in the Warnings window. A train JMRI isn't following, or a parked train on a detector that drops out, would otherwise open one at every Block. There, Nothing: Detector Stuck tells train tracking to take no notice of that Block's detector until it is trusted again, from the Warnings window or the Block dialog; nothing else that uses the sensor changes.

The Block dialog

On a Layout Editor panel in operating mode, right-click a Block's track and choose Block ...; or right-click a Block Contents icon (its double-click still edits the Block's value). The dialog shows the trains in the Block and how sure train tracking is of each. Why says how each train came to be there, and History lists the last 20 things that happened in the Block.

The Block Table

The Block Table has a Train column, showing the trains train tracking has in each Block, and a Why? column, both hidden until chosen from the column header's right-click menu. The Block's Value column is unchanged.

Operations

When a train has the same name as an Operations train that has been built, train tracking takes what the train is made up of from Operations. The train is divided into the parts it will be split into: each group of cars to be set out at the same stop, and what goes on to the end with the engines. Each part's length is worked out from its cars' and engines' lengths in Operations, at Operations' scale. Their total is the train's Operations length. Both are taken again each time the train moves on to its next stop. The Block dialog shows the train's work at its next stop: how many cars and engines it is to set out and pick up there.
